Foreshortening

A slope facing the radar is closer at the top than at the bottom.

Its whole length is squeezed into a few range bins, so it looks short and bright.

It happens when the slope toward the radar is below θ. It is strongest as the slope nears θ.

Layover

When the slope toward the radar is steeper than θ, the top is reached before the bottom.

The image order flips. The peak appears on the near-range side of its own base.

Hatched cells are passive layover. They are flat ground that shares range bins with the slope, so their signal is mixed.

Shadow

A slope facing away that is steeper than 90° − θ is never lit.

Ground behind a high ridge can also fall in shadow.

These cells return nothing but noise. Low θ gives more layover. High θ gives more shadow.
