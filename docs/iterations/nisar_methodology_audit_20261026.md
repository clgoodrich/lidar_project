# NISAR methodology and implementation audit — 2026-10-26

## Assessment

The saved local-decoy pad score is reproducible, but it does not establish a pad-specific radar signal independent of location. A coordinate-only classifier performs better on the same samples and folds. There are also substantive sampling, training-isolation, date-filtering, and phase-product issues. This is useful exploratory work; its stronger detection and physical-measurement claims need further validation.

This audit inspected the current working tree, including existing uncommitted changes. It did not alter scientific processing code, regenerate feature rasters, retrain U-Nets, or rerun every historical experiment. Historic model checkpoints may have been generated by earlier code; no claim is made that simulated training windows reproduce past training exposure exactly.

## High-priority findings

### 1. Local decoys have not removed location confounding

The coordinate-only result above is the most consequential finding. NISAR's local AUC cannot presently be interpreted as evidence of incremental information beyond geographic setting. NISAR fold AUCs were 0.998, 0.558, 0.613, 0.771 and 0.843; coordinate-only fold AUCs also varied dramatically. Pooled AUC alone conceals this instability.

Use spatially separated evaluation regions with a buffer, show fold sample counts and AUCs, and compare geography/terrain/context versus the same baseline plus SAR on identical held-out examples. A separate area is still needed for transfer. A local shuffled-label experiment alone would not substitute for these comparisons.

### 2. Equal-pixel sampling is not actually implemented

`_nisar_feat_score_9t.py:99–106` accepts targets with at least four core pixels, then takes at most N_DISC pixels. Decoys always have N_DISC geometric pixels.

Reconstructing the sample population from the annotations and grid gave exactly the saved 649 pads and 495 pits. Among matched targets:

- 473/626 pads (75.6%) have fewer than the decoy's 49 pixels; median target support is 35 pixels.
- 335/488 pits (68.6%) have fewer than the decoy's nine pixels; median target support is seven pixels.

The original sample-support concern remains. Geometric pixel counts are also insufficient when finite-value counts differ by band: targets require four finite samples, while decoy nanmedians have no corresponding minimum. The direction and magnitude of resulting AUC bias require a rerun; they cannot be inferred from this count alone.

Use translated target footprints or per-target identical sampling masks and matched valid support. Match controls on the actual pixels and aggregation used by the features. Currently matching uses full-core means, whereas scored controls and features use medians of the selected subset. This is particularly relevant to the unexpectedly strong pit lidar baseline even after roughness matching.

### 3. The U-Net sampler does not enforce held-out pixel isolation

`_dl.py:249–272` chooses centres from training pads or training block bounds, but reads the entire 384-pixel image and global-label window. Clipping is to image bounds only. `_pad_unet_cv5.py:313–319` passes training blocks to this sampler without a fold-specific label mask. A block assignment for the centre does not prevent supervised training on neighbouring held-out pixels.

Using saved fold assignments, current inner-validation splitting rules and the actual feature grid, 1,000 simulated background draws per fold produced 182, 192, 222, 211 and 214 windows intersecting held-out blocks. These are possible exposure rates under the current sampler, not a reconstruction of historical training batches. Centred/jittered draws were not included in this simulation.

Require the whole training footprint to stay inside allowed blocks, or enforce held-out feature/label exclusion and loss masking with an appropriate buffer. Retrain and reevaluate before using the near-1.0 U-Net AUC as a leakage-free ceiling. The downstream fusion also uses different outer folds from U-Net training; a rigorous stacked evaluation needs outer-fold isolation throughout base-model and fusion training.

### 4. A known bad date is still admitted by the default feature pipeline

`_nisar_area.py:86–88` only applies BAD_DATES for the lidar SLC stack or Warren. Default 9t GSLC processing checks valid fraction alone. The current index admits track 26 on 2026-09-08 despite that date being explicitly listed as bad. The other listed bad date fails the valid-fraction threshold.

The admitted counts are 10/11/15, coincidentally matching the document's reported counts without implementing its stated exclusion. Correct the shared filter and rebuild dependent products; cached results must not silently survive this change.

### 5. Block CV does not imply separation of neighbouring feature footprints

`_nisar_feat_model_9t.py:32` uses unbuffered GroupKFold. In the local-1,500 m set, 869/3,014 rows have a training centre within 100 m in their held-out fold; 375 have one within 50 m. Several features aggregate neighbourhoods, including 75 m texture windows and 505 m long-wave phase removal. Adjacent blocks can share substantial source support and landscape structure.

The local set's 2,740 decoy rows represent only 1,704 unique centres. The default set has 6,260 decoys at 3,741 unique centres. Exact duplicates share their spatial block, so this is not evidence that exact duplicates cross folds. It does reweight a smaller comparison population and invalidate treating all decoy rows as independent observations.

## Phase products and scientific interpretation

### 6. LOS outputs are not a defensible first-to-last displacement series

`_nisar_feat_los_motion_9t.py:41–60` skips long gaps, concatenates retained steps, substitutes zero for low-coherence steps, then cumulatively sums and fits an annual rate. The data contain a winter-to-summer gap. Disconnected temporal components have no measured relative displacement; a missing step is not zero movement. Midpoint timestamps also do not represent the endpoints of cumulative increments.

These bands may remain exploratory predictors, but should not be described as measured cumulative displacement or velocity. Build connected temporal networks, handle unwrapping/reference explicitly, retain missing intervals as unobserved, and validate with known or injected displacement. The 505 m spatial high-pass also removes real long-wavelength motion along with atmospheric/orbital structure. A noise estimate is not by itself a detection limit without injection/recovery and error-rate analysis.

### 7. Height-derived output has a unit bug and unresolved identifiability

`_nisar_feat_height_resid_9t.py:100` uses `ak = abs(k) * 0.01` but labels its fitted coefficient as coherence change per 0.01 rad/m. The predictor should be `abs(k) / 0.01` for that interpretation: the existing coefficient is 10,000 times the stated unit-scaled coefficient. This also changes numerical conditioning and the determinant threshold used to accept fits. Scaling alone need not change a tree classifier's rankings.

The hard-coded wavelength is 0.2384 m, whereas indexed centre frequency is 1.239 GHz, implying 0.241963 m. The ordinary interferogram code correctly derives this latter wavelength; the height code does not.

More fundamentally, a local high-pass wrapped phase/baseline regression with its sign selected to make dense forest positive cannot independently establish absolute canopy scattering height or demonstrate the expected height sign. Require identifiable baseline/time geometry, wrapping checks, deterministic sign convention, uncertainty and an independent height reference. Atmospheric/deformation effects and dependent pairs remain concerns.

### 8. Season and processing release are completely confounded in the indexed sample

All 11 October 2025–January 2026 acquisitions in the index are beta. All 26 June–October 2026 acquisitions are provisional. Consequently, winter-to-summer differences cannot distinguish season from processing release in these observations. A per-track offset does not solve a within-track release/season confound.

The document also incorrectly groups provisional products with uncalibrated beta data: ASF describes provisional products as fully calibrated and algorithmically improved. That does not imply every derived result is validated. Reprocess historical acquisitions consistently if available; otherwise limit seasonal interpretations to identifiable contrasts and explicitly characterize remaining artifacts. See [ASF known issues](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/product-known-issues/).

## Additional implementation and inference issues

- **Preprocessing leaks across folds.** Class-dependent missingness filtering and imputation occur before splitting (`_nisar_feat_model_9t.py:62–67` and related scripts). Both belong inside each training fold. The imputation-only diagnostic changed AUC very little here; it is a real protocol defect but not an explanation for the main score.
- **Single-band significance assumes independent observations.** `_nisar_feat_score_9t.py:48–50,164–167` independently bootstraps target and decoy rows and applies Mann–Whitney tests despite matched, overlapping, reused and spatially dependent samples. BH correction does not repair invalid underlying p-values. Use inference respecting spatial clusters and matched structure. HH/HV signed-rank tests across correlated bands similarly overstate the effective replication.
- **Skewness/kurtosis are mislabeled.** `_nisar_feat_spatial_9t.py:29–35` computes moments of a locally demeaned residual image, not each window's central moments. An executed linear-ramp check returns excess kurtosis approximately -3, versus the actual seven-column window value -1.25. Rename these descriptors or implement actual local central moments.
- **Reproduction instructions are incomplete.** The documented bare `_nisar_regeocode_lidar_9t.py` invocation defaults to `nisar_dem`, does not write, and reaches iteration over `a.v1=None`. Production needs the explicit lidar/write/coregistration settings and their prerequisite offset tables. Most feature scripts default to GSLC; height and sub-aperture use lidar-derived placement. Record per-product source stack and processing provenance instead of implying one uniform stack.
- **Feature discovery is implicit.** `read_all()` globs all matching products without asserting their CRS/transform/shape or checking a run manifest. Current inspected grids agree, but stale products, mixed source stacks and parameter changes can silently enter a later experiment. Save a manifest with source granules, exclusions, processing versions, transforms, code revision, parameters, band definitions and sample IDs.

## Remaining blind spots

1. **Specificity:** separating annotated pads from isolated ordinary ground is not identifying oil/gas infrastructure. Include logging landings, roads, driveways, building sites, quarries and other disturbed clearings. Road-near controls at 30–100 m still do not match pads with median road distance 7 m.
2. **Detection value:** use held-out detector candidates, measure precision/recall and false positives per square kilometre, and measure incremental SAR benefit at fixed recall or fixed review workload. Case-control ROC AUC does not establish useful deployment precision. The arbitrary 0.05 gain gate needs a paired uncertainty estimate and a practical objective.
3. **Ground truth and time:** 2019 lidar-derived annotations versus 2025–26 SAR can mix cleared, reforested, rebuilt or removed features. Audit temporal persistence independently. Incomplete annotations affect negatives; uncertain DEP locations and unknown equipment presence weaken both positive and null steel tests.
4. **Registration and resolution:** 5 m posting is not five-metre independent resolving power. Assess impulse response, effective looks, track-dependent registration and uncertainty relative to target size. Three-by-three geocoded samples do not establish nine independent looks. Repeat key analyses across plausible spatial shifts without selecting the best shift on test labels. The official [GSLC description](https://hyp3-docs.asf.alaska.edu/nisar-docs/gslc/) distinguishes posting, phase flattening and beta0 radiometry.
5. **Noise and terrain:** validate HV sensitivity to the noise floor, RFI, layover/shadow and per-date radiometric stability. Dual-pol HH/HV descriptors are useful but do not uniquely identify scattering mechanisms or metal. Sub-aperture splitting sacrifices resolution; equal pixel posting does not preserve equal effective support.
6. **Inference limits:** the data support “these experiments did not demonstrate reliable steel/well identification,” not a general assertion that NISAR cannot identify such targets. Likewise failure to improve this fusion benchmark is not evidence of no possible NISAR value.

## Recommended order

First fix sample support, date filtering, whole-footprint split isolation and fold-local preprocessing; freeze a versioned dataset. Then repeat a small preregistered amplitude/texture/coherence model against geography plus lidar/context controls under buffered validation. Add hard negatives and a held-out area. Only then expand phase-derived physical claims or feature searches. Independently rerun leakage-free U-Net training and evaluate SAR on its actual mistakes, where complementary information can be measured.
