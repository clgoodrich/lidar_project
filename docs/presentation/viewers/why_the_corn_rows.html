Share of half-metre map squares holding no return at all. Single-coverage ground only, so the flight-line overlap cannot flatter a column. 809,761 cells of 5 m across the whole tile.

Read down a column: trees roughly double it. Read across a row: the angle multiplies it thirty-fold. The recipe for a corn row is the middle-to-outer part of a single pass, before the neighbouring strip starts helping, preferably under trees.

What is measured, what is drawn, and what I got wrong

Measured from the delivered point cloud. Scan-line bearing 78.0°, from the ground pattern with nothing assumed — for each candidate bearing, project the returns onto the perpendicular and histogram them; real ruled lines spike, any other bearing smears. Corn-row bearing 79.0°, from the NoData mask, independently. Ground speed 70.1 m/s from the near-nadir track. Every percentage above.

From the manufacturer, not the file. The sensor. The LAS header names only the processing software. The rotating polygon, the dual channel and the ±14° cross-fire tilt all come from RIEGL's published scan-pattern description.

Three numbers I had wrong here before. A 3.00 m stripe spacing, from an autocorrelation told to ignore lags under 2 m — so of course it found something over 2 m. A ground speed of 112.2 m/s, from regressing position against time over all returns, which measures the sweep and not the aircraft. And "oscillating mirror, 54.3 Hz", because two interleaved channels make the scan angle look like it reverses. All three are withdrawn, along with a story about lines pairing up at the swath edge that was built on them. A polygon has no turnaround and nothing to pair.
